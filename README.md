# Tipoff

Every basketball game in one place: NBA, WNBA, NCAA M and NCAA W (football and baseball next). Live at **https://raylikeray.github.io/tipoff/**

- **Layout:** a command center per league. Pick a league in the top tabs (NBA · WNBA · NCAA M · NCAA W) and the home screen shows everything about it at once: today's scoreboard rail, tonight's call (the featured game with the AI read), standings or rankings, leaders, injuries with their line impact (or 7-day movers for college), power ratings, the model's record, and news. The section bar under the tabs opens the full pages: Scores, Standings, Rankings (college), Teams, Players, Forecasts, Injuries (NBA/WNBA), News, Record. Links are `#<league>/<section>`; old links still work.
- **Personal:** first visit opens a welcome picker (all four leagues in tabs) and an updates panel; later, "Pick my teams" on each league screen or the bell in the header reopen it. The picker is a logo grid (saved on the device only, no accounts). Your teams get a strip at the top with the next or live game, the forecast, the last result and who is out; their games go first in the scoreboard and their rows are highlighted. The site installs to the home screen (manifest + `sw.js`; an install strip appears once on phones). **Updates for my teams** (`js/app.js`, favorites only, while Tipoff is open or installed): pregame 30 minutes before tip with the forecast and who is out, close games late, finals, injury-report changes (checked every 15 min), and transactions (every 30 min). Each kind can be toggled; nothing is sent for other teams. True push with the app closed needs a small server and is not built. The written matchup pieces are labeled **Prediction** in the UI.
- **Live data:** ESPN's public site API, fetched in the browser. Scores refresh every 30 s while games are live; the league tabs carry a live count.
- **Forecasts:** margin-based Elo per league, rebuilt nightly by `tools/nightly.mjs ratings` (GitHub Action `nightly.yml`) into `data/<league>/ratings.json`; the browser adds home court, rest and an injury adjustment weighted by scoring (`js/core.js`). Every pregame call is scored against the result and ESPN's own pregame win probability in `data/<league>/record.json`, shown on the Record page.
- **The model learns:** `tools/nightly.mjs tune` runs after the ratings each night. It replays the whole season under a grid of home-court, rest, spread and rating-speed values, keeps the mix with the best Brier score, moves the live parameters part of the way there (capped per night so it learns gradually), fits a calibration layer once there is enough data, and rebuilds the ratings with the new mix. Results go to `data/<league>/params.json` with a history that the Record page shows as a tuning log; the section bar shows the latest change. Tuning starts after 60 NBA / 40 WNBA / 150 college games.
- **AI reads:** `context.yml` builds `ai/context-<date>.json` (up to 15 featured games) at ~8:17 AM CT; a scheduled Claude task writes `ai/<date>.json` at ~9:13 AM CT. The same context job grades yesterday's reads against the results into `ai/record.json` (overall, last 7 days, by league, by confidence, and how the AI did when it disagreed with the model) and feeds that record, with yesterday's hits and misses, back into the next morning's context so the reads can correct themselves.
- No build step. Edit `index.html`, `css/app.css`, `js/*.js` and push; bump the `?v=` on the asset links in `index.html` so browsers pick up changes.

## Design: Broadcast
Mark: a disc split by the line at center court, the top half already tipping off (`img/symbol.svg`; app icon `img/mark.svg`; PNG icons in `img/`). Two-tone: ink over orange on light, white over orange on dark.
Studio-gray ground, white panels, ink module headers with an angled orange tab, team abbreviations in their own color block, condensed numerals for scores and percentages, a red block for anything live. Barlow for text, Barlow Condensed for numbers and labels. Dark mode is the same system on near-black. All tokens are at the top of `css/app.css`; the 12-column module grid collapses to two columns on tablets and one on phones, where a sticky jump bar moves between modules.
